## Organizing Clinical Information

Introduction narration: Organize clinical information using SOAP, explore the patient's history and context, and use the review of systems to uncover patient-reported symptoms.

### SOAP and Data Types (`soap-and-data-types`)

1. **Organize information with SOAP** — soap-overview.png (scene)

   Source: Clinicals3_Module0_Illustrations_Set01.zip / 01_soap_overview.png

   Accessible description: Organize information with SOAP

   SOAP means Subjective, Objective, Assessment, and Plan. It separates the information you gather from your interpretation and next steps.

   Narration: A clear structure helps your listener follow your thinking. As you explore SOAP, ask whether each detail is a report, a finding, an interpretation, or an action.

2. **Subjective: the patient's report** — subjective-patient-report.png (scene)

   Source: Clinicals3_Module0_Illustrations_Set01.zip / 02_subjective_patient_report.png

   Accessible description: Subjective: the patient's report

   Subjective includes symptoms and history reported by the patient. Mr. Jones describes sharp pain that worsens with inspiration.

   Narration: Start by listening to the patient's experience. Even a precise pain rating is subjective when the patient reports it; a number alone does not make information objective.

3. **Objective: observed findings** — objective-exam-findings.png (scene)

   Source: Clinicals3_Module0_Illustrations_Set01.zip / 03_objective_exam_findings.png

   Accessible description: Objective: observed findings

   Objective includes what you observe or measure. Mr. Jones's localized lung crackles are an examination finding.

   Narration: Now separate what you found from what you were told. The source reports stable vital signs without numbers, so keep that wording rather than inventing measurements.

4. **Assessment: interpret the evidence** — 6b92c.svg (icon)

   Source: Approved Figma export; mobile/assets/module0/provenance.json / 6b92c.svg

   Accessible description: Decorative compact symbol; concept title/text supply its accessible meaning.

   Assessment connects the findings to your clinical judgment. Explain which possibilities the evidence supports and what remains uncertain.

   Narration: Your assessment is more than a repeated list of facts. Show the connection between the clues and your interpretation, while leaving room for alternatives.

5. **Plan: propose next steps** — 6b92c.svg (icon)

   Source: Approved Figma export; mobile/assets/module0/provenance.json / 6b92c.svg

   Accessible description: Decorative compact symbol; concept title/text supply its accessible meaning.

   Plan describes proposed actions and follow-up. Connect each next step to the assessment and discuss it with your preceptor.

   Narration: A proposed action should have a reason. Consider what information you still need and explain how the next step could address that gap.

6. **Put SOAP together** — 6b92c.svg (icon)

   Source: Approved Figma export; mobile/assets/module0/provenance.json / 6b92c.svg

   Accessible description: Decorative compact symbol; concept title/text supply its accessible meaning.

   Begin with reports and findings, then explain your interpretation and proposed actions. Keep each detail in the section that reflects its role.

   Narration: Try following the story from evidence to judgment to action. Can your listener tell which details came from the patient and which came from your examination?
